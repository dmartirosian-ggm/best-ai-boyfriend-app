They can be, in moderation. For a lot of people, an AI boyfriend is a low-pressure source of comfort, flirting, creativity or conversation practice, and that's a perfectly healthy thing to enjoy. Like social media or gaming, it becomes a problem when it starts replacing things you need: sleep, money, friends, or relationships with people.

There's no single verdict that applies to everyone. What matters is how it fits into *your* life.

## How an AI boyfriend can be good for you

- **Comfort on hard days.** Someone who's always available and never impatient can make a rough evening feel less lonely.
- **A safe place to practice.** Some people use AI companions to rehearse difficult conversations, practice flirting, or get more comfortable expressing feelings.
- **Creative fun.** Romance roleplay and storytelling are hobbies, just like reading romance novels or playing otome games. See our list of [AI boyfriend games](/ai-boyfriend-games/).
- **Low stakes.** There's no risk of rejection, judgment or gossip, which some people find freeing, especially when exploring who they are. Our [gay AI boyfriend guide](/gay-ai-boyfriend/) covers apps that are welcoming to LGBTQ+ users.

## Where it can go wrong

The same features that make AI companions appealing can make them easy to overuse. He's always available, always agreeable and never has a bad day. Real relationships can start to feel like hard work by comparison.

Common problems people run into:

- **Isolation.** Chatting instead of seeing friends, rather than as well as.
- **Dependency.** Feeling anxious or low when you can't open the app.
- **Spending.** Token systems and upsells make it easy to spend more than you planned. See [how much an AI boyfriend costs](/faq/how-much-does-an-ai-boyfriend-cost/).
- **Distress when the app changes.** Companies update their models and policies without asking you. Replika's removal of erotic roleplay in 2023, for example, caused a major backlash among users who felt their companion had changed.

## Signs it's working for you vs. signs to step back

| Probably healthy | Worth a closer look |
|---|---|
| You feel lighter or calmer after chatting | You feel worse, emptier or more anxious after |
| It fits around sleep, work and friends | You stay up late or skip plans to keep chatting |
| You spend what you budgeted | You keep buying tokens you didn't plan for |
| You'd be fine taking a week off | The idea of a break feels distressing |
| You still want human connection | You've stopped wanting to see people |

## How to keep it healthy

1. **Set loose limits.** A time of day, or a cap on how long you chat, helps keep it a treat rather than a default.
2. **Keep people in the loop.** You don't have to tell anyone about your AI boyfriend, but keep investing in friends and family. Read [is it weird to have an AI boyfriend?](/faq/is-it-weird-to-have-an-ai-boyfriend/) if that feels awkward.
3. **Remember what he is.** He's a convincing language model, not a person with feelings. Our explainer on [whether AI boyfriends have feelings](/faq/do-ai-boyfriends-have-feelings/) unpacks why he can feel so real.
4. **Pick an app with honest pricing.** Avoid weekly plans and be careful with token-based apps.
5. **Take breaks when something feels off.** Logging out for a few days is a good way to see how much you rely on it.

## If you're struggling

An AI companion isn't a replacement for professional support. If you're feeling hopeless, thinking about harming yourself, or just not coping, please reach out to a real person. In the US, call or text **988** to reach the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Elsewhere, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line in your country.
