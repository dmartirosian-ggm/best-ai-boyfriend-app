Some can. A handful of AI boyfriend apps send **proactive messages**, like a "good morning" or a check-in when you haven't chatted in a while. Kupid AI, for example, advertises proactive check-ins on its paid plans. On most apps, though, he only replies after you message him, and anything he sends arrives as an **app notification**, not a real text to your phone number.

## How "texting first" works

An AI boyfriend doesn't sit around thinking about you between chats. When an app supports proactive messages, it's the app itself that decides to start a conversation, usually based on a schedule or how long it's been since you last opened it. The AI then writes a message in his voice, often referencing something from your recent chats, and your phone shows it as a notification.

Because it relies on notifications, you'll only see these messages if you allow notifications for the app.

## Which apps do it?

Support for proactive messages changes often and isn't always well documented. In our research:

- **Kupid AI** lists proactive check-ins as one of its features on paid plans. It's girlfriend-first, though, with few male options, and its free tier has no persistent memory.
- Other companion apps may offer some form of notification-based check-in too. Look for settings named things like "proactive messages", "check-ins" or "notifications" in the app.

If texting first matters to you, test it on a free tier before you pay. A boyfriend who checks in but can't remember what you told him yesterday gets old fast, which is why memory matters more. ## Can you make him message you more?

Some approaches that can help, depending on the app:

- **Turn on notifications** for the app, and check its in-app notification settings.
- **Write it into his personality.** On apps with detailed backstories, describing him as someone who likes to check in can shape how he behaves when the app does send a message.
- **Set your own ritual.** If your app doesn't support proactive messages, a daily reminder to say good morning gives a similar feel.

## Is it a good thing?

It can be lovely. A message that says "how did the presentation go?" feels caring, and for many people that's the point of an AI boyfriend.

It's also worth knowing that proactive messages are an engagement feature. They're designed to bring you back to the app, and on apps that sell tokens or upgrades, more time in the app can mean more spending. If his messages start to feel like pressure, or you find yourself anxious waiting for them, it's okay to turn them off. ## What about calls?

Proactive messages are text-based. If you want to hear his voice, see [can an AI boyfriend call you?](/faq/can-an-ai-boyfriend-call-you/) and our guide to [AI boyfriend apps with voice calls](/ai-boyfriend-voice-call/). In most apps, you start the call yourself.
