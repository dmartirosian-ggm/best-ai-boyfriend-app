No, it's not weird. AI companion apps have become a mainstream kind of app, and people use AI boyfriends for all sorts of ordinary reasons: comfort after a long day, a bit of flirting, creative roleplay, or a low-pressure way to practice talking about feelings. The more useful question isn't "is this weird?" but "is this good for me?"

## Why people have AI boyfriends

There's no single type of person who uses these apps. Common reasons include:

- **Company.** Someone to chat with in the evening who remembers how your interview went.
- **Fun and fantasy.** Romance roleplay is a hobby, like reading romance novels, writing fan fiction or playing dating sims. Our list of [AI boyfriend games](/ai-boyfriend-games/) covers the more game-like options, and [anime AI boyfriends](/anime-ai-boyfriend/) covers the anime side.
- **Safe exploration.** Some people use AI companions to explore their identity or what they want in a partner without judgment. Our [gay AI boyfriend guide](/gay-ai-boyfriend/) lists apps that are welcoming to LGBTQ+ users.
- **Practice.** Rehearsing a hard conversation, building confidence, or getting used to expressing affection.
- **Curiosity.** Plenty of people simply want to see what the technology can do.

## It's a new version of something old

People have always formed attachments to characters: in novels, TV shows, video games and fan communities. Interactive fiction, otome games and virtual pets were all once considered odd and are now just hobbies. An AI boyfriend is a more responsive version of the same impulse. He talks back, remembers details and adapts to you, which is exactly why it can feel more intense.

## When it's worth a second look

The question of "weird" usually hides a better one: is this healthy for me? It's worth checking in with yourself if:

- you're choosing the app over people you'd normally see,
- you feel anxious or low when you can't chat,
- you're spending more than you planned on tokens or upgrades, or
- you're keeping it secret because *you* feel uneasy about it, not just because it's private.

None of these mean you've done something wrong. They're just signals to adjust. Our guide on [whether AI boyfriends are healthy](/faq/are-ai-boyfriends-healthy/) goes deeper, and [AI boyfriend vs. real boyfriend](/faq/ai-boyfriend-vs-real-boyfriend/) looks at what each can and can't offer.

## Dealing with other people's reactions

Not everyone will get it, and that's okay. A few things that can help:

- **You don't owe anyone an explanation.** It's your hobby and your time.
- **Frame it in familiar terms if you want to share.** "It's like an interactive romance novel" lands better than a long defense.
- **If you're in a relationship, talk about it.** Couples draw lines in different places. An open conversation beats a surprise.
