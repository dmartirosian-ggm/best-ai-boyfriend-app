If you've come across Muah AI and wondered **is Muah AI safe**, here's our direct answer: **no, we don't recommend it.** In 2024, Muah AI suffered one of the most serious data breaches in the AI companion category, exposing about 1.9 million accounts and linking people's email addresses to what they had typed.

This review explains what happened, what Muah AI offers, and which apps to use instead if you want an AI boyfriend without that risk.

## What is Muah AI?

Muah AI is an uncensored AI companion service available on the web, with mobile apps also reported. It offers text chat, voice messages, voice calls, image generation and custom characters, and its most expensive plan reportedly adds real phone calls (US only).

It's marketed mainly as an **AI girlfriend** app. You can create custom characters, so a male companion is possible, but the product isn't built around boyfriends.

We couldn't verify who operates Muah AI or when it launched. For an app that handles explicit conversations, that lack of transparency is a concern in itself.

## The 2024 Muah AI data breach

This is the most important part of this review.

### What happened

In **September 2024**, data from roughly **1.9 million Muah AI accounts** was exposed. According to reporting at the time:

- The data included **email addresses linked to the prompts** users had written for sexual image generation.
- **Some of those prompts described child abuse**, which made the breach a major news story.
- The breach was reported by **404 Media** and added to **Have I Been Pwned**, the widely used breach-notification service.
- **Extortion attempts** against people whose data was exposed were reported afterwards.

We're not going to describe the content further. The key point for you is simple: many people's private, intimate prompts were tied to real email addresses, and some were then targeted.

### Why it matters

Every AI companion app stores what you type. Most people accept that because they trust the company to protect it. Muah AI's breach showed what happens when that trust fails: conversations that people assumed were private became something that could be used against them.

The breach also raises questions about the platform's moderation, given what some of the exposed prompts contained. And because breached data tends to keep circulating online, the risk to affected users didn't end when the news cycle moved on. That's one reason we weigh breach history so heavily in our scores: it's one of the few safety signals you can verify from the outside.

### Was your account affected?

If you ever used Muah AI:

1. **Check your email** at Have I Been Pwned, which lists the Muah breach.
2. **Change your password** anywhere you reused it, and turn on two-factor authentication.
3. **Don't pay anyone** who threatens to expose your data. Report it to the platform the message came from and, if you feel threatened, to the police.
4. **Consider deleting your account** and using a different email for any future companion apps.

## What to look for in a safer companion app

The Muah AI breach is a useful checklist for judging any AI boyfriend app before you sign up:

- **A known operator.** Can you find the company name and the country it's based in? Apps under strict data laws, such as the EU's GDPR, at least have a regulator you can complain to.
- **A clean breach record.** Search the app's name plus "breach" and check Have I Been Pwned.
- **A readable privacy policy.** Look for what's stored, whether chats are used to train AI, and how to delete your account.
- **Account deletion that works.** You should be able to delete your data, not just the app.
- **Sensible payments.** Clear prices and a recognizable checkout, not a lookalike site.

None of this guarantees safety, but it filters out the riskiest apps. More in [are AI boyfriends safe?](/faq/are-ai-boyfriends-safe/)
