## What is Roger AI?

We searched the web and AI tool directories for a companion app called Roger AI. Here's what turned up:

- **No AI boyfriend or companion app** called Roger AI that we could verify.
- **One unrelated product called Roger AI**: a screen-guidance assistant for Windows and macOS that helps you with tasks on your computer. It launched on Product Hunt in April 2026 and has a paid Pro plan. It is a productivity tool, not a companion or romance app.

So if you were hoping to download "the Roger AI boyfriend app", there doesn't appear to be an established one.

## Why are people searching for "Roger AI boyfriend"?

We couldn't find a source that explains the trend, so we can't say for certain. The most likely explanations are:

1. **A user-made character.** Character-chat platforms such as [Character AI](/reviews/character-ai/), [Talkie AI](/reviews/talkie-ai/) and [PolyBuzz](/reviews/polybuzz/) host huge libraries of user-created characters. A popular male character named Roger could easily drive searches like this.
2. **A small or new clone app.** App stores have many tiny companion apps with generic names that come and go quickly.
3. **Social media.** A video or post mentioning an AI boyfriend called Roger could send people searching for the app.
4. **Confusion with the productivity tool** that shares the name.

If you know where "Roger" comes from, you can usually find him by searching for the character's name inside the app where he lives, rather than looking for a separate app.

## Be careful with apps and sites claiming to be Roger AI

When a search term grows quickly and there's no clear official product, it's an opportunity for scammers. Lookalike sites have already targeted real companion brands, such as the fake Lovescape domain we cover in our [Lovescape review](/reviews/lovescape/). Treat anything calling itself the "Roger AI boyfriend app" with caution.

<Callout type="warning" title="Red flags for fake AI boyfriend apps">
- A website offering an **APK download** or a "premium unlocked" version.
- An app with **no clear developer name**, company or privacy policy.
- **Very few reviews**, or lots of short, similar five-star reviews posted at once.
- Requests to pay with **gift cards, crypto** or through a messaging app.
- Pressure to buy a **"lifetime" deal** right now.
- Asking for **unusual permissions**, such as your contacts or SMS.
</Callout>

## How to check that an AI boyfriend app is legit

Before you download any companion app, take two minutes to run through this checklist:

1. **Check the developer.** In the App Store or Google Play, tap the developer name. A legitimate app usually has a real company behind it, and often other apps too.
2. **Look for a privacy policy and terms.** If you can't find who runs the app or what it does with your chats, skip it.
3. **Read recent reviews.** Sort by newest and look for complaints about billing, fake features or the app not working.
4. **Search the app's name plus "scam" or "breach".** It takes seconds and can save you a lot of trouble.
5. **Download only from official sources**: the App Store, Google Play or the company's own website, typed in yourself.
6. **Start free.** Use the free tier before paying, and pay monthly rather than annually until you're sure.
